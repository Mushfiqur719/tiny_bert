\begin{abstract}
Clickbait headline detection in low-resource Indic languages, particularly Bengali, has predominantly relied on heavy pre-trained language models (such as BanglaBERT with $\sim$110.6M parameters). While these large models achieve strong classification accuracy, their significant parameter footprint ($\sim$423 MB), high CPU latency ($>$120 ms), and memory requirements prevent deployment on resource-constrained client environments, such as browser extensions and mobile devices. In this work, we propose \textbf{TinyBanglaBERT-4L}, an ultra-compact 4-layer student transformer model (\textbf{7.42M parameters, 14.91$\times$ compression}) trained via temperature-scaled knowledge distillation and strided pre-trained layer initialization ($L_S = \{2, 5, 8, 11\}$) with factorized embeddings.

Extensive empirical evaluations through a $3 \times 2$ factorial ablation and depth-scaling study demonstrate that TinyBanglaBERT-4L retains \textbf{96.59\% of the Teacher's Macro-F1} (0.7895 vs. 0.8174) and \textbf{96.19\% of accuracy} (80.54\% vs. 83.73\%) on the in-domain BanglaBait test set ($N = 1,506$), and \textbf{96.64\% Macro-F1 retention} (0.7768 vs. 0.8038, 80.09\% accuracy) on the strictly deduplicated leak-free test subset ($N = 1,351$). On CPU edge hardware, TinyBanglaBERT-4L combined with ONNX Runtime dynamic INT8 quantization compresses the model to \textbf{7.29 MB} (\textbf{57.99$\times$ smaller} than the Teacher), achieving a single-headline latency of \textbf{4.00 ms} (\textbf{30.83$\times$ faster}, \textbf{249.9 headlines/second}) while improving test accuracy to \textbf{80.81\%} and Macro-F1 to \textbf{0.7917} (\textbf{96.86\% Teacher retention}) with zero quantization loss. Holm-Bonferroni corrected exact McNemar tests confirm that 3$\times$ layer compression from 12 to 4 layers incurs no statistically significant error increase ($p = 0.6592$), while dual SHAP and LIME attributions verify lexical grounding. Furthermore, on an unseen zero-overlap cross-domain benchmark of 10,000 human-annotated headlines (\textit{BaitBuster-Bangla}), TinyBanglaBERT-4L exhibits a \textbf{34\% smaller domain degradation} than the Teacher ($-4.36\%$ vs. $-6.60\%$ Macro-F1), retaining \textbf{99.27\% of Teacher Macro-F1} (0.7459 vs. 0.7514) and outperforming the 110.6M Teacher in Clickbait F1 (0.7288 vs. 0.7253, \textbf{100.48\% retention}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Clickbait headline detection in Bengali poses significant computational challenges due to morphological richness and colloquial social-media phrasing. While 110.6M-parameter models like BanglaBERT~\cite{b1} achieve high accuracy, their 422.76 MB footprint and $>120$ ms CPU latency preclude real-time execution inside browser extensions and mobile applications. This paper introduces \textbf{TinyBanglaBERT-4L}, systematically comparing random initialization, orthogonal PCA teacher projection ($768 \to 256$, 11.55M parameters), and strided pre-trained compact transfer (7.42M parameters) across 4, 6, and 12 encoder layers under knowledge distillation~\cite{b2,b3}.

\section{Methodology}
The distillation objective combines class-weighted Cross-Entropy ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$) with temperature-scaled Kullback-Leibler divergence ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}$):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \alpha \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}\left(\sigma(z_S), y\right) + (1 - \alpha) T^2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}\left(\sigma\left(\frac{z_S}{T}\right), \sigma\left(\frac{z_T}{T}\right)\right)
\end{equation}
Grid search optimization on the validation split selects $T=4.0, \alpha=0.1$ for PCA-projected initialization ($W_p \in \mathbb{R}^{768 \times 256}, W_p^T W_p = I$ with neutral LayerNorm $\gamma=\mathbf{1}, \beta=\mathbf{0}$) and $T=4.0, \alpha=0.5$ for strided compact transfer ($L_S = \{2, 5, 8, 11\}$ with factorized embeddings $d_e=128 \to d_h=256$).

\subsection{Selective Prediction \& Two-Tier Edge--Cloud Cascade}
Using validation-fitted temperature scaling ($T_{\text{cal}}=1.7375$), TinyBanglaBERT-4L achieves an Area Under the Risk--Coverage Curve (AURC) of \textbf{0.10268}, reaching \textbf{83.69\% accuracy (0.8199 Macro-F1) at 90\% coverage}, \textbf{85.98\% at 80\% coverage}, \textbf{90.38\% (0.8905 Macro-F1) at 60\% coverage}, and peaking at \textbf{97.50\%} on high-confidence predictions (Table~\ref{tab:selective}). In a two-tier Edge--Cloud cascade where TinyBanglaBERT-4L (4.00 ms INT8) handles 80\% of headlines locally and defers the most uncertain 20\% to the Teacher, cloud requests drop by 80\% while achieving \textbf{83.20\% system accuracy (0.8145 Macro-F1)} at 6.52 ms amortized latency (and \textbf{0.8177 Macro-F1} at 60\% edge offload, outperforming the standalone Teacher).

\section{Conclusion}
TinyBanglaBERT-4L achieves a 14.91$\times$ parameter reduction (7.42M parameters), a 57.99$\times$ disk size reduction (7.29 MB in INT8), and a 30.83$\times$ CPU speedup (4.00 ms/headline, 249.9 fps) over BanglaBERT while preserving 96.86\% in-domain Macro-F1 (80.81\% accuracy) and 99.27\% cross-domain Macro-F1 (100.48\% Clickbait F1), enabling real-time edge deployment.
